\section{Abstract}

Classical \textit{Schrödinger Bridge} (SB) problem seeks a path measure that minimally deviates from a given reference measure subject to prescribed initial and terminal marginals. This works investigate numerical approach to dynamic of interacting brownian particle with normal reflection term in low-dimensional setting ($\displaystyle d\leqslant 3$) on bounded domain $\displaystyle \mathcal{D} \subset \mathbb{R}^{d}$. To alleviate the difficulty due to nonlinearity incurred by non-local coupling, a nested fixed-point is adopted with Sinkhorn-type iterates. Further, fully semi-lagrangian discretization is used to propagate the solution of forward-backward partial differential equations (FB-PDEs).

\section{Related Works}

Interest in spatially-constrained SB is largely under-developed, especially when also incorporating mean-field framework. This work is inspired by \cite{Caluya2020ReflectedBridge, Eldesoukey2026MeanFieldSinkhorn}. First paper studies SB with reflected dynamic with Sinkhorn iterate where the time marching solution involve Neuman and Rudin boundary conditions, and show great performance in low-dimension. The latter generalized standard Sinkhorn to accept partial-integro-PDEs associated with mean-field SB resulting in nested version. Along this line \cite{Adu2026ReflectedSchrodinger} studies degenerate, sub-Riemannian dynamics, and use Sinkhorn simiarl to the former. Recent work \cite{LiuKalise2026} use Finite element discretization to solve the weak form of coupled PDEs for reflected SB. \cite{Deng2024ReflectedGenerative} derives forward-backward SDEs associated coupled FB-PDEs of reflected SB and extend divergence-based likelihood training in smooth bounded domain and shown to scale in high-dimension. Discrete-time framework also been explored with chance-constrants \cite{Rapakoulias2023DiscreteCovariance, Rapakoulias2025MeanFieldGMM} with mixture of Guassian marginal contraints. Alternative method such as introducing direct soft-penalty for particle escaping region exists as demonstrated in \cite{Liu2022DeepGSB} using FB-SDE representation. There is a direct link between system of optimality of SB and MFG, therefore one can naturally adopt a scheme in MFG such as finite-difference to solve SB problem. \cite{CalzolaCarliniDupuisSilva2023, CarliniCoscetti2026, CarliniSilva2013} adopt a semi-lagrangian approach. 


\section{Problem Formulation}


Let $\displaystyle \mathcal{D} \subset \mathbb{R}^{d}$ and boundary $\displaystyle \partial \mathcal{D}$, represented by smooth level-set $\displaystyle \chi ( t,x)$ via $\displaystyle \mathcal{\chi } < 0$ on $\displaystyle \mathcal{D}$ and $\displaystyle \chi =0$ on $\displaystyle \partial \mathcal{D}$. Assuming $\displaystyle \nabla \chi \neq 0$ on $\displaystyle \partial \mathcal{D}$, the outward unit normal $\displaystyle \mathbf{n}( x)$ is obtained by $\displaystyle \mathbf{n} =\nabla \chi /\Vert \nabla \chi \Vert $. Denote by $\mathscr{P} (\mathcal{S} )$ the space of probability measures on $\mathcal{S}$ endowed with the $2$-Wasserstein metric $W_{2} (\cdot ,\cdot )$ and having finite 2nd-moment. Let $\displaystyle \Omega \coloneq \mathcal{C} (\mathcal{T} ;\mathbb{R}^{d} )$ where $\displaystyle \mathcal{T} \coloneq [ 0,T]$ be canonical path space. Let $\displaystyle \mathbb{P}_{t} \in \mathscr{P} (\mathbb{R}^{d} )$ be $\displaystyle t$-time pushforward marginal of $\displaystyle \mathbb{P} \in \mathscr{P} (\Omega )$. For given fixed $\displaystyle \mathbb{P}$ and $\displaystyle \mu _{\text{init}} \in \mathscr{P} (\mathbb{R}^{d} )$, consider the dynamic of weakly-interacting brownian particle modeled by Mckean-Vlaskov reflected SDEs: \ 
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
dX_{t} =-\textstyle ( \nabla E\star \mathbb{P}_{t})( X_{t}) +\sigma \ dW_{t} -\mathbf{n}_{t} dV_{t} & &  & & ,X_{0} \sim \mu _{\text{init}}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}which induces the path law $\displaystyle \Gamma (\mathbb{P}) \coloneq \text{Law}\left(( X_{t})_{t\in \mathcal{T}}\right)$, $\displaystyle W$ is $\displaystyle \mathbb{R}^{d}$-valued Wiener on $\displaystyle (\si{\ohm},\mathcal{F} ,\mathbb{F} =(\mathcal{F}_{t} )_{t\in \mathcal{T}} ,\mathbb{P}_{0} )$ Here $\displaystyle E$ is potential, $\displaystyle {\textstyle ( \nabla E\star \mu )( \cdot ) \coloneq \int _{\mathbb{R}^{d}} \nabla E(\cdot -y)\ \mu (dy).}$ And $V$ is a continuous, non-decreasing, $\displaystyle \mathbb{F}$-adapted bounded-variation process satisfying $\displaystyle {\textstyle V_{0} =0}$, $\displaystyle {\textstyle \int _{0}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{s} \notin \partial \mathcal{D}_{s} \}} \ dV_{s} =0}$, and $\displaystyle {\textstyle V_{t} =\int _{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{s} \in \partial \mathcal{D}_{s} \}} \ dV_{s}}$. For simplicity takes diffusion be $\displaystyle \sigma \equiv \sqrt{2\kappa }\mathbb{I}_{d}$ for $\displaystyle \kappa _{0}  >0$. In \cite{} shows that the rate function governing the large deviations for mean-field type dynamic in (1) is $H(\mathbb{P} \parallel \Gamma (\mathbb{P} ))$. Unlike standard non-mean-field SB in that the referenc measure is no longer fixed and the convexity property is losed due to nonlinear dependency in its arguments of relative entropy \cite{}. Under appropriate hypotheses, the existence of $\displaystyle \mathbb{F}$-progressively measurable $\displaystyle \alpha ( t,x)$ is guaranteed by Girsanov's, 
the relative entropy can be parametrized by process $\displaystyle \alpha $:
\begin{equation}
{ H\bigl(\mathbb{P} \parallel \Gamma (\mathbb{P} )\bigr) =\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\left[\int _{0}^{T} \| \alpha (t,X_{s} )\| ^{2} \ ds\right]}
\end{equation}
and that $\displaystyle X_{t}$ follows the controlled Mckean Vlasov SDE:
\begin{equation}
dX_{t} =b^{\alpha } (t,X_{t} ,\mathbb{P}_{t} )\ dt+\sigma _{t} \ dW_{t}^{\mathbb{P}} -\mathbf{n}_{t} dV_{t} ,\ \ X_{0} \sim \mu _{\text{init}} ,
\end{equation}where $W^{\mathbb{P}}$ is a standard $(\mathbb{P} ,\mathcal{F}_{t} )$-Brownian, $\displaystyle b^{\alpha } (t,x,\mu )\coloneq \sigma _{t} \alpha _{t} -{\displaystyle \nabla E\star \mu }$. Further, under additional hypothesis, the perturbation is markovian feedback control. i.e. there exists $\displaystyle \Phi _{t} \in H_{-1}( \rho _{t} :t\in \mathcal{T})$ such that $\displaystyle \alpha _{t} =\Phi _{t}( X_{t})$, $\displaystyle dt\times d\mathbb{P}$ a.s. (here assuming the control dimension is similar to state dimension). Following \cite{}, using transformed control \ $\displaystyle m:=\rho \alpha \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$, rewrite (2) 
as SOC problem
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{J}_{\text{rmf-sb}}^{*} =\inf_{(\alpha ,\rho )}\mathcal{J}_{\text{rmf-sb}} (m,\rho ) ,\ \text{where} \ \mathcal{J}_{\text{rmf-sb}}( m,\rho ) \coloneq { \frac{1}{2}\int _{0}^{T}\int _{\mathbb{R}^{d}} \| m_{t} \| ^{2} /\rho _{t} dx\ dt.}
\end{equation}The law of sample path $\displaystyle ( X_{t})_{t\in \mathcal{T}}$ in (3) can be characterized as solving the martingale problem, i.e. under suitable assumption, for any $\displaystyle \psi \in \mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathcal{D})$, the process 
\begin{equation}
{\textstyle M_{t} \coloneq \psi ( X_{t}) -\psi ( X_{0}) -\int _{0}^{t}\mathcal{G}[ \rho _{s}] \psi ( X_{s}) ds}
\end{equation}is $\displaystyle \mathbb{P}$-local martingale with respect to $\displaystyle \mathbb{F}$ where $\displaystyle \mathcal{G}$ is generator for (3) defined by $\displaystyle \mathcal{G}[ \rho ] \coloneq \kappa \nabla \rho +\nabla \rho \cdot ( \sigma \alpha -E\star \rho )$. It follows that \cite{}, the marginal flows $\displaystyle ( \rho _{t})_{t\in \mathcal{T}}$ is a weak solution of forward Kolmogorov equation:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\partial _{t} \rho +\nabla \cdot J =0 & ,\ ( t,x) \in \mathcal{D} ,\\
J\cdot \mathbf{n} =0 & ,\ ( t,x) \in \partial \mathcal{D}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where probability flux is $\displaystyle J( m,\alpha ) =\sqrt{2\kappa } m-\rho \nabla E\star \rho -\kappa \nabla \rho $, where here using $\displaystyle m\coloneq \rho \alpha $. One can now characterize the 1st-order optimality condition associated with (4) with contraint (6) which yields a system of forward-backward PDEs. Here the system is adjacent to that appear in \cite{}, supplemented with additional Neuman and Robin boundary conditions. The proof given here is straightforward application of Lagrange mulitplier on Banach space. 



\subsection{Assumption}
\begin{itemize}
\item The domain $\displaystyle \mathcal{D} \subset \mathbb{R}^{d}$ is sufficiently regular. 
\item Interacting term $\displaystyle E\in \mathcal{C}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d} ;\mathbb{R}\right)$ is symmetric, i.e. $\displaystyle E( \cdot ) =E( -\cdot )$ with $\displaystyle \sup\nolimits _{z,v\in \mathbb{R}^{d}} v^{\top } \nabla ^{2} E( z) v< +\infty $. 
\item The admissible controls $\displaystyle \alpha \in \Theta _{\alpha } \coloneq H^{1}( \rho )$ s.t. $\displaystyle \exists \psi \in H^{1}$ with $\displaystyle \alpha =\nabla \psi $, $\displaystyle | m| ^{2} /\rho \in L^{1}$, and $\displaystyle \rho \in \mathcal{C}\left(\mathcal{T} ,\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{D})\right) \cap L^{2}\left( 0,T;H^{1}(\mathcal{D})\right)$ with $\displaystyle \rho \geqslant 0$, $\displaystyle {\textstyle \int _{\mathcal{D}} \rho =1}$ and $\displaystyle \rho ( 0,\cdot ) dx=\mu _{\text{init}}$, $\displaystyle \rho ( T,\cdot ) dx=\mu _{\text{targ}}$
\end{itemize}

The rest of this work focus solving the following coupled FB-PDEs which can be drived in straightforward manner by mean of Lagrange Multiplier on Banach space. The difference between (PDE-opt) and that which given by \cite{Backhoff2020MeanFieldErgodic} is the extra boundary condition.
\begin{proposition}
The optimal control $\displaystyle \alpha ^{*} ,\rho ^{*}$ are given by $\displaystyle \alpha ^{*} =\sqrt{2\kappa } \nabla \log \varphi $ where $\displaystyle ( \phi ,\rho )\rightarrow ( \varphi ,\hat{\varphi }) \coloneq ( e^{-\phi } ,\rho \exp( E\star \rho /\kappa +\phi )$ solve the following FB-PDEs:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\begin{cases}
-\partial _{t} \varphi =\left(\mathcal{A}^{\rho } +\mathcal{R}^{\rho }\right)[ \varphi ] & \\
\partial _{t}\hat{\varphi } =\left(\mathcal{A}^{\rho ,\dagger } +\mathcal{R}^{\rho ,\dagger }\right)[\hat{\varphi }] & 
\end{cases} ,\  & \text{on} \ {\displaystyle (0,T)\times \mathcal{D} ,} \  & \text{such that} \  & \begin{cases}
\nabla \varphi ^{\top }\mathbf{n} =\nabla \hat{\varphi }^{\top }\mathbf{n} =0,\ \text{on} \ ( 0,T) \times \partial \mathcal{D}\\
\mu _{\text{init}} =W[ \rho ] \varphi _{0}\hat{\varphi }_{0} ,\ \text{on} \ \{0\} \times \mathcal{D}\\
\mu _{\text{targ}} =W[ \rho ] \varphi _{T}\hat{\varphi }_{T} ,\ \text{on} \ \text{\{T\}} \times \mathcal{D}
\end{cases} & 
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\displaystyle \mathcal{A}^{\rho }[ \eta ] \coloneq ( \nabla E\star \rho ) \cdot \nabla \eta -\kappa \Delta \eta $ is advection-diffusion operator and $\displaystyle {\textstyle \mathcal{R}^{\rho }[ \eta ] \coloneq -\eta \nabla E\star ( \rho \nabla \log \eta )}$ is nonlocal source term, and $\displaystyle W[ \rho ]( t,x) \coloneq e^{-E\star \rho /\kappa }$.
\end{proposition}




\subsection{Fixed-Point Iterates}
The constructive scheme for the PDE-opt solver is obtained via a single-loop Picard-Sinkhorn iteration to find the fixed point of the flow $\rho = (\rho_t)_{t\in\mathcal{T}} \in \mathcal{C}(\mathcal{T}\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{D}))$. We define the marginal map $F$ and the inner propagator $G$ by
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
F[\rho; \varphi, \hat{\varphi}] \coloneq W[\rho]\varphi\hat{\varphi} \quad \text{and} \quad G[\varphi_T; \mu, \varphi, \hat{\varphi}] \coloneq \left( M_T^{\mu} \mathcal{S}_{0\to T}^{\dagger, \mu, \hat{\varphi}} \circ M_0^{\mu} \mathcal{S}_{T\to 0}^{\mu, \varphi} \right)[\varphi_T].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}%
The propagator $G$ shares a very similar structure with the classical linear Schrödinger bridge (SB), which is a well-studied numerical method~\cite{Chen2021SinkhornReview, leonard2012from}. Here, $\mathcal{S}_{0\to T}^{\dagger, \mu, \hat{\varphi}}$ and $\mathcal{S}_{T\to 0}^{\mu, \varphi}$ are respectively the backward and forward time-split propagators of the linearized PDE-opt system frozen at $(\mu, \varphi, \hat{\varphi})$. The term ``linearized PDEs'' refers to the fact that under fixed $(\rho^{n-1}, \varphi^{n-1}, \hat{\varphi}^{n-1})$, both the nonlocal drift $b_t^{n-1} \coloneq -\nabla E \ast \rho_t^{n-1}$ and the reaction rate $r_t^{n-1} \coloneq -\nabla E \ast (\rho_t^{n-1} \nabla \log \varphi_t^{n-1}) \in L^\infty(\mathcal{D})$ are frozen in time and space, reducing the nonlinear forward-backward PDEs to standard non-autonomous linear Cauchy problems with the time-varying generator $\mathcal{A}_t^{n-1} + \mathcal{R}_t^{n-1}$. It follows that this generator induces a unique backward evolution family $(\mathcal{P}_{t\to s}^{n-1})_{0 \leqslant s \leqslant t \leqslant T}$ on $L^2(\mathcal{D})$; see also~\cite{BatkaiCsomosFarkasNickel2011}. Here, $M_T^{\mu}$ and $M_0^{\mu}$ are Sinkhorn scaling operators defined pointwise by
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\hat{\varphi}_0(\cdot) \xleftarrow{M_0^{\mu}} \frac{\mu_{\text{init}}(\cdot)}{W[\mu](\cdot, 0)\varphi(\cdot, 0)} \quad \text{and} \quad \hat{\varphi}_T(\cdot) \xleftarrow{M_T^{\mu}} \frac{\mu_{\text{targ}}(\cdot)}{W[\mu](\cdot, T)\varphi(\cdot, T)}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}%
Note that these two operators are similar to those in~\cite{Eldesoukey2026MeanFieldSinkhorn}. We suppress the superscripts $\rho^{n-1}, \varphi^{n-1}$ for readability. For a uniform temporal subdivision $0 = t_0 < t_1 < \dots < t_{N_T} = T$ of step size $\Delta t = t_{k+1} - t_k$, we define the exact continuous symmetric Strang splitting $\mathcal{S}_{t_{k+1}\to t_k}$ acting on $\varphi$ via the composition
\begin{equation}
\varphi(t_k, \cdot) \approx \mathcal{S}_{t_{k+1}, t_k}[\varphi](t_{k+1}, \cdot) \coloneq \left(\mathcal{R}_{t_{k+\Delta t/2}\to t_k} \circ \mathcal{U}_{t_{k+1}\to t_k} \circ \mathcal{R}_{t_{k+1}\to t_{k+\Delta t/2}}\right)[\varphi](t_{k+1}, \cdot).
\end{equation}%
Since $\mathcal{R}[\eta](s,x) = r_s(x)\eta(x)$ is a bounded pointwise multiplication operator on $L^2(\mathcal{D})$, it admits an analytical solution of the form $\mathcal{R}_{t\to s}[\eta](x) = \eta(x)\exp\int_s^t r_u(x)\,du$, whose half-step approximations via quadrature points are given by
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{k+1\to k+\Delta t/2}[\eta] \coloneq \eta e^{\Delta t r_{t_{k+1}}/2} \quad \text{and} \quad \tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{k+\Delta t/2\to k}[\eta] \coloneq \eta e^{\Delta t r_{t_k}/2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}%
Next, the middle operator $\mathcal{U}_{t_{k+1}\to t_k}$ solving the advection-diffusion subproblem has the Feynman-Kac representation~\cite{milstein2000numerical} over $[t_k, t_{k+1}]$ with Neumann boundary conditions given by
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{U}_{t_{k+1}, t_k}[\eta](x) \coloneq \mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\left[\eta(t_k, X_{t_k}) \mid X_{t_{k+1}} = x\right] = \lim_{K\to\infty} \sum_{1 \leqslant m \leqslant K} w_m \eta\left(t, X_{t_k, t_{k+1}}^x(\omega_m)\right),
\end{equation}%
where $X_{t_k, t_{k+1}}$ solves (for fixed $\rho$) the forward process starting from $X_{t_k} = x$, and $w_m$ are normalized positive weights. Due to random noise incurred by finite approximations of expectations via Monte Carlo (of order $O(\sqrt{\Delta t / K})$), a deterministic cubature stencil is used instead, known as the Semi-Lagrangian (SL) method. Defining $\mathcal{S}_{T\to 0} \coloneq \mathcal{S}_{t_1\to t_0} \circ \mathcal{S}_{t_2\to t_1} \circ \dots \circ \mathcal{S}_{t_{N_T}\to t_{N_{T-1}}}$, it is guaranteed~\cite{BatkaiCsomosFarkasNickel2011} that a second-order error bound for sufficiently smooth terminal data $\varphi_T$ holds; i.e., there exists $C \geqslant 0$ independent of $\Delta t$ such that
\begin{equation}
\|\mathcal{P}_{0,T} \varphi_T - \mathcal{S}_{T\to 0} \varphi_T\|_{L^2} \leqslant C \Delta t^2 \|\varphi_T\|_{L^2}.
\end{equation}%
Similarly, since the Fokker-Planck equation for $\hat{\varphi}$ is the formal $L^2$-adjoint of the backward equation, $\mathcal{S}_{k\to k+1}^\dagger \coloneq (\mathcal{S}_{k+1\to k})^\dagger$ is obtained simply by reversing the operation:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\varphi}(t_{k+1}, \cdot) \approx \mathcal{S}_{t_k\to t_{k+1}}^\dagger[\hat{\varphi}](t_k, \cdot) = \left(\mathcal{R}_{t_{k+1}\to t_{k+\Delta t/2}}^\dagger \circ \mathcal{U}_{t_{k+1}\to t_k}^\dagger \circ \mathcal{R}_{t_{k+\Delta t/2}\to t_k}^\dagger\right)[\hat{\varphi}](t_k, \cdot).
\end{equation}%
Notice that $\mathcal{R}_{t\to s}^\dagger = \mathcal{R}_{t\to s}$ is self-adjoint. Upon spatial discretization, this structure ensures the discretized forward propagator $\mathcal{S}_{0\to T}$ can be obtained via mass-weighted matrix transpose of $\mathcal{S}_{T\to 0}$; see also~\cite{eldesoukey2026schrodingerbridgeskineticswarming}.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\DontPrintSemicolon
\KwIn{$\mu_{\text{init}}, \mu_{\text{targ}} \in \mathbb{R}_{>0}^{N_x}$, $\Delta t = T/N_T$, $\theta \in (0, 1]$, threshold $\epsilon > 0$.}
\textbf{Initialize:} $\boldsymbol{\varphi}_k^0, \hat{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}_k^0 \leftarrow \mathbb{1}_{N_x}$ and $\boldsymbol{\rho}^0 \leftarrow \mathbb{1}_{N_x} / \operatorname{Vol}_d(\mathcal{D})$ for $k = 0, \dots, N_T$.
\For{$n = 1, \dots, N_{\text{iter}}$}{
    Compute $\mathbf{V}_k^{n-1}, \mathbf{W}_k^{n-1}, \mathbf{R}_k^{n-1}, \mathbf{r}_k^{n-1}, \mathbf{b}_{k,l}^{n-1}$ for $k = 0, \dots, N_T$\;
    Set $\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{N_T}^n \leftarrow \boldsymbol{\varphi}_{N_T}^{n-1}$\;
    \For{$k = N_T - 1, N_T - 2, \dots, 0$}{
        $\boldsymbol{\varphi}_k^n \leftarrow \mathbf{R}_k^{n-1} \mathbf{\Pi}_k^{n-1} \mathbf{R}_{k+1}^{n-1} \boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k+1}^n$\;
    }
    Set $\hat{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}_0^n \leftarrow \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{init}} \oslash \left(\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{W}_0^{n-1}) \odot \boldsymbol{\varphi}_0^n\right)$\;
    \For{$k = 0, \dots, N_T - 1$}{
        $\hat{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}_{k+1}^n \leftarrow \mathbf{R}_{k+1}^{n-1} \mathbf{\Pi}_k^{n-1,\dagger} \mathbf{R}_k^{n-1} \hat{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}_k^n$\;
    }
    Set $\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{N_T}^n \leftarrow \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{targ}} \oslash \left(\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{W}_{N_T}^{n-1}) \odot \hat{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}_{N_T}^n\right)$\;
    Compute $\boldsymbol{\rho}_k^{1/2} = \operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{W}_k^{n-1}) \odot \boldsymbol{\varphi}_k^n \odot \hat{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}_k^n$ and normalize in $L^1(\mathcal{D}_\Delta)$: $\boldsymbol{\rho}_k^{1/2} \leftarrow \boldsymbol{\rho}_k^{1/2} / (\mathbf{1}^\top M_{\text{lump}} \boldsymbol{\rho}_k^{1/2})$\;
    Set $\boldsymbol{\rho}_k^n = (1-\theta)\boldsymbol{\rho}_k^{n-1} + \theta\boldsymbol{\rho}_k^{1/2}$ for $k = 0, \dots, N_T$\;
    \If{$\max_{1 \leqslant k \leqslant N_T-1} \|\boldsymbol{\rho}_k^n - \boldsymbol{\rho}_k^{n-1}\|_\infty \leqslant \epsilon$}{
        \textbf{break}\;
    }
}

\caption{Fixed-Point Iteration Solver}
\end{algorithm}

Unlike the nested two-loop scheme in~\cite{Eichinger2026SinkhornConvergence, eldesoukey2026second, eldesoukey2026schrodingerbridgeskineticswarming}, Solver 1 executes only a single loop, and the nonlocal drift and reaction terms are updated alongside the scaling operators $M_0$ and $M_T$. This structure closely mirrors fictitious play and Picard iteration in mean-field games (MFG)~\cite{Inoue2023FictitiousPlay} with extended Sinkhorn scaling applied to PDEs. The next section concerns spatial discretization via a monotone $P_1$ finite element Semi-Lagrangian scheme.

\DontPrintSemicolon
\For{$n = 1, \dots, N_{\text{iter}}$}{
    $\varphi^n \leftarrow \mathcal{S}_{T\to 0}\left[\varphi_T^{n-1}; \rho^{n-1}, \varphi^{n-1}\right]$ \tcp*{backward propagate}
    $\hat{\varphi}_0^n \leftarrow M_0^{\rho^{n-1}}\left[\varphi^n\right]$ \tcp*{initial marginal scale}

}

\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Semi-Lagrangian Discretization}
What remains now is spatial discretization via SL. As mentioned in~\cite{CalzolaCarliniDupuisSilva2023}, the SL scheme is monotone, unconditionally stable, and positivity-preserving without requiring the parabolic CFL condition $\Delta t = O(\Delta x^2)$. Let $\mathcal{D}_\Delta = \{x_i\}_{1 \leqslant i \leqslant N_x} \subset \overline{\mathcal{D}}$ be the vertices of a simplicial triangulation of size $\Delta x$ over the polyhedral domain $\mathcal{D}$, and let $\mathcal{T}_\Delta = \{t_k = k\Delta t\}_{0 \leqslant k \leqslant N_t}$ be the time subdivision. At $t_k$, the continuous field $\eta \in L^2(\mathcal{D})$ is approximated by nodal values $\boldsymbol{\eta}_k = (\eta_{k,1}, \dots, \eta_{k,N_x}) \in \mathbb{R}^{N_x}$. Its continuous reconstruction is obtained via the $P_1$ finite element interpolator $\mathcal{I} : \mathbb{R}^{N_x} \to \mathcal{C}_b(\mathcal{D})$ defined by $\mathcal{I}[\eta](\cdot) \coloneq \boldsymbol{\beta}(\cdot)^\top \boldsymbol{\eta}$, where $\boldsymbol{\beta} = (\beta_1, \dots, \beta_{N_x})^\top$ is the vector of standard piecewise linear Lagrange basis functions satisfying $\beta_i(x_j) = \delta_{ij}$ and $\mathbf{1}^\top \boldsymbol{\beta} = 1$. This interpolation introduces a spatial discretization error strictly bounded by $O(\Delta x^2)$. Following~\cite{CarliniSilva2013}, we choose $\beta_i(x) = \max\{0, 1 - |x - x_i|/\Delta x\}$, which has support on $[x_i - \Delta x, x_i + \Delta x]$. The choice of linear interpolants (yielding $O(\Delta x^2)$ error) over higher-order alternatives such as Hermite polynomials or splines (which achieve spatial errors up to $O(\Delta x^4)$) is motivated by properties endowed by $\mathcal{I}$, such as positivity preservation, monotonicity, and computational efficiency.%

We approximate $\mathcal{U}_{t_{k+1}\to t_k}$ with a second-order weak $(2d+1)$-point stencil; see~\cite{CalzolaCarliniDupuisSilva2023}. For node $x_i \in \mathcal{D}_\Delta$ at time $t_k$, the advective transport under reference drift $\mathbf{b}_{k,i}^{n-1} \coloneq (-\nabla E \star \rho_k^{n-1})(x_i) \in \mathbb{R}^d$ with diffusivity $2\kappa \mathbb{I}_d$ is approximated by the unconstrained backward characteristic path over $[t_k, t_{k+1}]$ via
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
y_{k,i,l}^s(a) = x_i - \Delta t \mathbf{b}_{k,i}^{n-1} + e_l s \sqrt{6\kappa d \Delta t}, \quad s \in \{0, \pm 1\}, \; l \in \{1, \dots, d\},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}%
where $e_l$ is the canonical basis vector. Neumann reflection boundary conditions are enforced for any characteristic exiting $\mathcal{D}$, following~\cite{CalzolaCarliniDupuisSilva2023} for the special case of normal reflection. Specifically, recalling that $\chi < 0 \Longleftrightarrow x \notin \mathcal{D}$ denotes the signed distance function, the Skorokhod map reads
\begin{equation}
\hat{y}_{k,\mathbf{i},\ell}^s \coloneq \begin{cases}
y_{k,\mathbf{i},\ell}^s & \text{if } y_{k,\mathbf{i},\ell}^s \in \mathcal{D}, \\
y_{k,\mathbf{i},\ell}^s - 2\chi(y_{k,i,l}^s) \mathbf{n}(\chi(y_{k,i,l}^s)) & \text{if } y_{k,\mathbf{i},\ell}^s \notin \mathcal{D},
\end{cases}
\end{equation}%
where $\mathbf{n}(\chi(a)) = \nabla\chi(a)/\|\nabla\chi(a\|_2$. Using these positions and three stencil weights, the fully discrete SL operator approximated at $x_i$ is given by the transition matrix $\mathbf{\Pi}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{N_x \times N_x}$ via $\mathcal{U}_{t_{k+1}\to t_k}[\boldsymbol{\eta}](x_i) \approx \sum_{1 \leqslant j \leqslant N_x} (\mathbf{\Pi}_k^{n-1})_{i,j} \eta_j$, where
\begin{equation}
(\mathbf{\Pi}_k)_{a,b} \coloneq \frac{2}{3} \beta_j(\hat{y}_{k,i,1}^0) + \frac{1}{6d} \sum_{\ell = 1}^d \left( \beta_j(\hat{y}_{k,i,\ell}^+) + \beta_j(\hat{y}_{k,i,\ell}^-) \right).
\end{equation}%
This choice achieves second-order accuracy. For the increment $\Delta Y_{k,i} = Y_{k,i} - x_i$, we have $\mathbb{E}[\Delta Y_{k,i}] = -\Delta t \mathbf{b}_k^{n-1}(x_i)$ and $\operatorname{Cov}(\Delta Y_{k,i}) = 2\kappa \Delta t \mathbb{I}_d + O(\Delta t^2)$, which is congruent with $\mathbb{E}[dX_t]$ and $\operatorname{Cov}(\sqrt{2\kappa} dW_t)$ up to second order, respectively. Furthermore, by construction, the matrix $\mathbf{\Pi}$ is row-stochastic (i.e., non-negative with $\mathbf{\Pi}^\top \mathbf{1} = \mathbf{1}$). For a probabilistic interpretation of the SL scheme in terms of controlled Markov processes, see~\cite{CalzolaCarliniDupuisSilva2023}. Finally, let $M_{\text{lump}} = \operatorname{diag}(m_1, \dots, m_{N_x})$ with mass weights $m_i = \int_{\mathcal{D}} \beta_i(a)\,da > 0$, and let $\mathbf{D}_l \in \mathbb{R}^{N_x \times N_x}$ be the lumped-mass discretized partial derivative $\partial_{x_l}$ along $l \in \{1, \dots, d\}$ defined by $(\mathbf{D}_l)_{i,j} = m_i^{-1} \int_{\mathcal{D}} \beta_i(x)\partial_{x_l}\beta_j(x)\,dx$.%

The discretized objects are summarized below for iteration $n-1$ (dropping superscripts for clarity), step $k$, $l \in \{1, \dots, d\}$, and $i \in \{1, \dots, N_x\}$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
(\mathbf{V}_k)_i &\coloneq \sum_j E(x_i - x_j)\rho_{k,j} m_j, & \mathbf{W}_k &\coloneq \operatorname{diag}\left(\exp(-\mathbf{V}_k/\kappa)\right), \\
\mathbf{R}_k &\coloneq \operatorname{diag}\left(e^{\Delta t \mathbf{r}_k/2}\right), & \mathbf{r}_k &\coloneq -\sum_{l,j} \rho_{k,j} m_j \partial_{x_l} E(x_i - x_j)^\top \frac{(\mathbf{D}_l \boldsymbol{\varphi}_k)_j}{\varphi_{k,j}}, \\
(\mathbf{b}_{k,l})_i &\coloneq -\sum_j \partial_{x_l} E(x_i - x_j)\rho_{k,j} m_j. & &
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}%
With respect to the lumped $L^2(\mathcal{D}_\Delta)$ inner product $\langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle_{M_{\text{lump}}} \coloneq \mathbf{u}^\top M_{\text{lump}}\mathbf{v}$, the discrete adjoint of the single-step backward matrix operator $\mathbf{S}_{k+1\to k} \coloneq \mathbf{R}_k \mathbf{\Pi}_k \mathbf{R}_{k+1}$ is given explicitly by
\begin{equation}
(\mathbf{S}_{k+1\to k})^\dagger = M_{\text{lump}}^{-1} (\mathbf{S}_{k+1\to k})^\top M_{\text{lump}} = \mathbf{R}_{k+1} \mathbf{\Pi}_k^\dagger \mathbf{R}_k,
\end{equation}%
where $\mathbf{\Pi}_k^\dagger \coloneq M_{\text{lump}}^{-1} \mathbf{\Pi}_k^\top M_{\text{lump}}$ due to commutation with $M_{\text{lump}}$.
